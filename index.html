Gaussian Tolerance Envelopes (PASM)

PASM avoids sample-relative min-max distortion by measuring candidate site values against absolute physical planetary envelopes:

Single-Parameter Similarity:
s_k(x_k) = exp( - 0.5 * ((x_k - T_k) / σ_k)² )
Composite Score:
S(x) = 100 * ( Σ w_k · s_k(x_k) ) / ( Σ w_k )
✓ 100% Perfect Match Guarantee: If an Earth site exactly matches target conditions, its score equals 100.0%.
✓ Sample Invariance: Adding 100 new candidate sites causes 0.00% change in existing analog scores.
✓ Tolerance Envelope (σ): A deviation of 1σ yields ~60.6% similarity; a deviation of 2σ yields ~13.5%.

Planetary Analog Similarity Model (PASM) Methodology

Rigorous physical envelope modeling ensuring sample invariance and exact mathematical reproducibility.

NASA Formula Clarification

NASA does not mandate or publish a single universal scalar equation to score Earth-to-planetary similarity. While NASA documents qualitative criteria and mission-specific evaluation frameworks, scalar similarity indexes are mathematical modeling choices.

Standard sample-relative min-max scaling suffers from a critical flaw: adding or removing a site shifts every other site's score. To eliminate this distortion, PASM introduces physically bounded Gaussian tolerance envelopes.

Scientific Limitations & Planetary Disclosures

  • 2m Air Temp vs Surface Skin Temp: The Moon has no atmosphere; on Mars, the 6 mbar boundary layer produces a 20-30°C thermal gradient between the soil and 2m air.
  • Aridity vs Vacuum: Terrestrial hyper-aridity cannot replicate the absolute desiccating vacuum of space.
  • Spatial Resolution: NASA POWER climatology spans ~50 km cells; microclimatic pockets (caves, shadowed craters) are averaged over this grid.
